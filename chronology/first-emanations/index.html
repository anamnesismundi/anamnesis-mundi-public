Pleroma · Sequence 1/3

The First Emanations

Barbelo and Autogenes

The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), one of the central texts of the Nag Hammadi Library, presents the Invisible Spirit, also called the One or the Monad, as the supreme source from which the divine order unfolds. The divine Fullness emerging from this source is known as the Pleroma. Its first sequence centers on Barbelo, the first manifestation of divine Thought, and Autogenes, the Child who follows her in the unfolding. Their appearance opens the passage from the transcendent source toward an ordered divine world.

The first movement begins as thought crosses the threshold into presence. Ennoia, the Father’s thought, becomes reality and appears in shining light as Pronoia, Barbelo. She gives glory to the Invisible Spirit through whom she has come forth. The passage carries thought from inwardness into encounter—from its origin in the source to a luminous presence able to answer and praise. At this threshold, the narrative turns from the appearance of Thought toward the coming forth of the Child.

When Autogenes is revealed, the language preserves two closely joined meanings. The Greek term reflected in the Coptic may be heard as chrēstos, “good,” and christos, “anointed.” The passage holds both resonances together: goodness shapes the Child’s completion, while anointing marks his place within the divine order. Through Autogenes, thought made present becomes effective divine agency, and the Pleroma begins to open toward the orders that follow.
