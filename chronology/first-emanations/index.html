Pleroma · Sequence 1/3

The First Emanations

Barbelo and Autogenes

The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), one of the central texts of the Nag Hammadi Library, reveals the Invisible Spirit, the One or the Monad, as the transcendent source from which the divine order unfolds. From this hidden source emerges the divine Fullness known as the Pleroma. Its first unfolding begins with Barbelo, the first manifestation of divine Thought, and continues with Autogenes, the Child who comes forth after her. In their appearance, what lies beyond all form and measure begins to disclose itself as an ordered divine world.

The first movement begins when thought passes from hiddenness into presence. Ennoia, the Father’s thought, comes forth in radiant light as Pronoia, Barbelo. Turning toward the Invisible Spirit from whom she has arisen, she gives glory to her source. What was held inwardly within the unknowable now becomes luminous presence, capable of beholding its origin and answering it in praise. From this first encounter between source and manifestation, the unfolding continues toward the coming forth of the Child.

With the appearance of Autogenes, two meanings gather around his nature. The Greek words preserved behind the Coptic allow the name to resonate as both chrēstos, “good,” and christos, “anointed.” The two belong together: goodness speaks to the perfection of the Child, while anointing marks his place within the emerging divine order. Through Autogenes, the thought first revealed in Barbelo enters into creative power, and the Pleroma opens further into the luminous orders that follow.
