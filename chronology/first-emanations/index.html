The First Emanations

Barbelo and Autogenes

Barbelo and Autogenes form the first articulated sequence after the Invisible Spirit in The Secret Book of John. This page examines the language through which the source expresses their place in the divine fullness and distinguishes that account from the related, but differently structured, traditions preserved in other Sethian texts.

The language of the unfolding

Coming forth, standing with and attending

“The First Emanations” is an editorial title used to identify this chronological sequence. The source itself describes the divine order through a series of relations: a power comes forth, another stands with it, a request is granted, a light is brought to perfection, and divine powers attend and give glory.

This language does not present the Pleroma as a material place populated through physical reproduction. Nor does it offer a simple family tree. It describes an ordered fullness in which divine realities are revealed through relation, likeness, consent and participation.

Barbelo is the image of the Invisible Spirit without being another name for the Spirit. Autogenes comes forth through Barbelo while receiving perfection and authority from the Spirit. Their identities are therefore expressed through their relations to one another and to the transcendent source.

Titles and distinctions

The compound identity of Barbelo

The designations applied to Barbelo should not be reduced to a single modern definition. First Thought and Forethought express her relation to the Invisible Spirit. Image identifies a relation of likeness. Mother-Father and Triple-Male express an androgynous fullness that cannot be described as exclusively feminine or exclusively masculine.

The source nevertheless speaks of Barbelo with feminine pronouns. Its feminine and androgynous language coexist and should not be made to cancel one another.

Forethought must also remain distinct from Foreknowledge. Forethought belongs to the source’s characterization of Barbelo, while Foreknowledge is a distinct power that comes forth and stands with her.

Autogenes, the Only-Begotten and the Christ

Autogenes, the Only-Begotten and the Christ designate the same divine figure within this sequence, but each term emphasizes a different relation.

“Autogenes,” commonly rendered “Self-Generated” or “Self-Begotten,” is the figure’s principal name within the wider Sethian tradition. “Only-Begotten” expresses his relation to the Mother-Father, while “Christ” identifies his position within the sacred order revealed by the text.

The name Autogenes should not be interpreted as meaning that he appears independently of every other divine reality. In The Secret Book of John, his manifestation remains inseparable from Barbelo and from the action of the Invisible Spirit. Likewise, the title Christ belongs here to a Sethian cosmological setting and should not automatically be defined through later orthodox Christology.

The parallel texts

Related names within different structures

Three Forms of First Thought

Three Forms of First Thought gives First Thought a revelatory voice. She speaks in the first person and manifests through Thought, Voice and Word. The text illuminates the active and descending character of primordial Thought, but it does not reproduce the Barbelo–Autogenes sequence of The Secret Book of John.

Allogenes and Zostrianos

Allogenes and Zostrianos place Barbelo and Autogenes within more elaborate contemplative and metaphysical structures. Barbelo can designate a supreme aeonic domain, while Autogenes appears as a divine level or order encountered in the soul’s ascent. These texts show the wider importance of the two names within Sethian tradition, but their hierarchies must not be inserted into The Secret Book of John as though all three works described one identical system.

The Gospel of Judas

The Gospel of Judas preserves another distinct configuration. A luminous cloud appears in the boundless realm of the great Invisible Spirit, and from it emerges the divine Self-Generated. Four angels then come forth to attend him. This is a close structural parallel to the relation between Autogenes and four attendant powers, but The Gospel of Judas does not narrate the complete generation through Barbelo found in The Secret Book of John. Its account remains an independent cosmological formulation.

Textual basis

The four witnesses of the Secret Book of John

The Secret Book of John survives in four Coptic manuscripts. The four witnesses preserve the central relation between the Invisible Spirit, Barbelo and Autogenes, while differing in wording and in some details of their titles and actions. This page follows the shared narrative structure and does not silently combine recension-specific expressions.
