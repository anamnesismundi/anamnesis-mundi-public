Sophia’s Independent Generation

The rupture within the Pleroma

In The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), one of the central works of the Nag Hammadi Library, the Pleroma is the divine Fullness, the luminous order that unfolds from the Invisible Spirit. Within its final movement stands Sophia, Wisdom, at the threshold where the harmony of the Fullness is about to be broken.

Sophia desires to bring forth something from herself, without the consent of the Spirit and without the participation of her consort. Yet the power within her gives form to the thought. What emerges bears the mark of this separation. It is imperfect, unlike its mother, a being born outside the harmony through which the divine order had until then unfolded.

When Sophia beholds what her desire has brought forth, it takes the form of a serpent with the face of a lion, its eyes flashing like bolts of lightning. She casts it away from herself, beyond the realm of the immortals, and surrounds it with a luminous cloud so that it may remain hidden from them. Within the cloud she places a throne. The being born from her solitary act she names Yaldabaoth.

With his birth, a rupture enters the unfolding. Sophia perceives that her own light has diminished, and in that loss she comes to recognize what has taken place. She begins to move to and fro, her outward movement revealing an inward displacement. Wisdom, having acted apart from the harmony of the Fullness, now turns toward what has been lost.

This turning becomes repentance. Her supplication rises through the Pleroma and is heard by the whole Fullness. A blessing descends upon her, and the Holy Spirit pours upon her from above. Sophia is raised beyond the being she has produced, while the deficiency born from her act begins its passage toward restoration. Her return is not yet complete, but the movement has changed: what began in separation now turns toward reunion with the divine order.
