Origin

The Monad

“The Monad,” “The Unity,” and “The One” are names given in modern English translations to the supreme principle revealed in the Sethian Gnostic text The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), one of the central works of the Nag Hammadi Library, preserved in three of its codices and in the Berlin Codex. Beneath these different names lies the same ineffable source, the Invisible Spirit from whom all being proceeds.

The revelation opens upon the whole mystery of existence: what is, what was, and what is to come, what is invisible and what is visible. John, the son of Zebedee, is entrusted with carrying these teachings to his spiritual companions, “who are from the unshakable generation of the perfect human.”

Beyond every boundary by which existence might be known or measured, The One is neither corporeal nor incorporeal, neither great nor small. It belongs neither to the aeons nor to time. Nothing precedes it. No measure can contain it, no name can encompass it, no word can fully utter it. Beyond perfection, blessedness and divinity, it is the inexhaustible source from which life, knowledge, goodness, mercy, redemption and grace arise.

Within the stillness of this unknowable source, thought becomes the first movement of revelation. From the Father’s thought, Barbelo comes forth as the First Thought, or Forethought, Pronoia, the image of the Invisible Spirit and the first power to appear. She is the first to emerge after the Monad, opening the primordial unfolding through which the hidden fullness begins to reveal itself.
