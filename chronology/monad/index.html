Origin

The Monad

“The Monad,” “The Unity,” and “The One” are English renderings used in modern translations of the Sethian Gnostic text The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), preserved in the Nag Hammadi codices and the Berlin Codex. Each rendering designates the supreme, transcendent source described throughout the text as the Invisible Spirit.

The revelation opens across the whole span of being — what is, what was and what is to come, what is invisible and what is visible. John is entrusted with carrying these teachings to his spiritual friends “who are from the unshakable generation of the perfect human.”

Beyond every boundary by which existence may be known or measured, The One is neither corporeal nor incorporeal, neither large nor small, and belongs neither to the aeons nor to time. Nothing precedes it that could limit, fathom, measure, name or utter it. Greater than perfection, blessedness or divinity, it is the source that gives life, knowledge, goodness, mercy, redemption and grace.

From the Father’s thought, Barbelo comes forth as the First Thought or Forethought — Pronoia — the image of the Invisible Spirit and the first power to appear. She is the first to come forth after the Monad, opening the first movement of the divine unfolding.
