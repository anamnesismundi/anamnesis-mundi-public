Origin

The Monad

“The Monad,” “the Unity,” and “the One” are English renderings used in major translations of The Secret Book of John for the supreme source described as the Invisible Spirit. Frederik Wisse uses “the Monad,” Marvin Meyer uses “the One,” while Karen L. King renders the shorter recension as “the Unity” and the longer recension as “the Monad.”

Translation history

Names across the translations

Frederik Wisse’s translation opens with “The Monad” and identifies it as God and Father of everything, the Invisible One and the Invisible Spirit. Marvin Meyer uses “The One,” followed by the titles God and Parent, Father of the All, the Invisible One and the Invisible Spirit. Karen L. King preserves the distinction between the surviving forms of the text, rendering the opening term as “the Unity” in the shorter recension and “the Monad” in the longer recension.

Manuscript transmission

The four surviving witnesses

The Secret Book of John survives in four Coptic manuscripts. Nag Hammadi Codices II and IV preserve the longer recension. Nag Hammadi Codex III and the Berlin Codex preserve the shorter recension. The two forms differ in wording and arrangement, including the way the opening designation is rendered in English.

Longer recension
NHC II,1 NHC IV,1
Shorter recension
NHC III,1 BG 8502,2

Wisse’s translation presents a single continuous text drawing on all four witnesses. King’s edition and translation keep the longer and shorter recensions visible separately, making the difference between “the Unity” and “the Monad” easier to observe.

Source vocabulary

Terms found in the source

The Invisible Spirit

The principal designation used throughout the cosmological account for the supreme source.

The Invisible One

A closely related designation emphasizing that it cannot be seen or contained among perceptible beings.

The Invisible Virginal Spirit

A designation appearing particularly in the account of Barbelo and the subsequent divine unfolding.

The Father of the All

The source from which Barbelo, Autogenes and the higher aeonic order ultimately proceed.

Pure and immeasurable light

The text describes it as pure light upon which no eye can gaze and as immeasurable light beyond comprehension.

Pronoia and Barbelo

Pronoia, Forethought, is the first Thought and image of the Invisible Spirit. Barbelo is the first power to appear from this Thought. These terms are related to the Monad but are not alternative names for it.

Valentinian comparison

Bythos in Valentinian cosmology

Bythos, the Depth, stands at the beginning of the Valentinian Pleromatic genealogy. In the Valentinian system reported by Irenaeus, Bythos is paired with Ennoea or Sige and precedes the subsequent emanation of the aeons. It belongs to a distinct Valentinian structure and enters Anamnesis Mundi as a comparative term, not as one of the designations used by the Sethian witnesses for the Invisible Spirit.
