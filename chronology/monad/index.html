Sethian
Origin

Monad

The Monad is the ultimate principle of divine reality: ungenerated, incorruptible, immeasurable and beyond comprehension. Nothing precedes or contains it. From its self-contemplation, the first divine unfolding begins with Barbelo.

Before the Pleroma

The transcendent source

In the Sethian cosmological framework represented here, the Monad stands beyond the ordered divine realms and beyond every lower cosmological structure. The Secret Book of John describes the supreme source through apophatic language: invisible, immeasurable, incorruptible, ineffable and beyond comprehension. Rather than existing as one being among others, it is the ultimate transcendent source from which the higher divine order unfolds.

Why this page says “Monad”

Monad is the concise editorial name used by Anamnesis Mundi for this position in the map. The surviving Sethian texts speak primarily of the Invisible Spirit; they do not use “Monad” as one uniform historical title.

Comparative boundary

Bythos — The Depth

Bythos belongs particularly to Valentinian cosmological terminology. It remains in the comparative layer and is not presented as a direct Sethian title for the Invisible Spirit.

Philosophical parallel

The Platonic and Middle-Platonic One

Middle-Platonic and later Platonic traditions describe a transcendent first principle above ordinary predicates; in later Platonism, differentiated levels of reality proceed from the One. This provides an important philosophical comparison with the Sethian Invisible Spirit, while the two should not be treated as identical concepts.

Alcinous, The Handbook of Platonism 10 · Plotinus, Enneads V.1–2 · John D. Turner, Sethian Gnosticism and the Platonic Tradition
Mythic parallel

Atum and Heliopolitan Cosmogony

In the Heliopolitan Egyptian cosmogony, Atum stands at the beginning of the divine genealogy and generates differentiated divine beings from himself. The comparison lies in the movement from primordial unity toward divine multiplicity; Atum remains a cosmogonic deity within Egyptian mythology and is not an equivalent of the radically transcendent Sethian Monad.

The Pyramid Texts, Utterance 527 · Richard H. Wilkinson, The Complete Gods and Goddesses of Ancient Egypt — Atum
