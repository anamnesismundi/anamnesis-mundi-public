Sethian Gnostic
Origin

The Monad

“The Monad,” “The Unity,” and “The One” are English renderings used in modern translations of the Sethian Gnostic text The Secret Book of John (The Apocryphon of John), preserved in the Nag Hammadi codices and the Berlin Codex. Each rendering designates the supreme, transcendent source described throughout the text as the Invisible Spirit.

Translation history

Names across the translations

Frederik Wisse’s translation opens with “The Monad” and identifies it as God and Father of everything, the Invisible One and the Invisible Spirit.

Marvin Meyer uses “The One,” followed by the titles God and Parent, Father of the All, the Invisible One and the Invisible Spirit.

Karen L. King preserves the distinction between the surviving forms of the text, rendering the opening term as “the Unity” in the shorter recension and “the Monad” in the longer recension.

Source vocabulary

Terms found in the source

The Invisible Spirit

The principal designation used throughout the cosmological account for the supreme source.

The Invisible One

A closely related designation emphasizing that it cannot be seen or contained among perceptible beings.

Pure and immeasurable light

The text describes it as pure light upon which no eye can gaze and as immeasurable light beyond comprehension.

The Invisible Virginal Spirit

A designation appearing particularly in the account of Barbelo and the subsequent divine unfolding.

The Father of the All

The source from which Barbelo, Autogenes and the higher aeonic order ultimately proceed.

Pronoia and Barbelo

Pronoia, Forethought, is the first Thought and image of the Invisible Spirit. Barbelo is the first power to appear from this Thought. These terms are related to the Monad but are not alternative names for it.

Manuscript transmission and translations

The four surviving witnesses and modern translations

The Secret Book of John survives in four Coptic manuscripts. Nag Hammadi Codices II and IV preserve the longer recension; Nag Hammadi Codex III and the Berlin Codex preserve the shorter recension. These witnesses form the textual basis for the modern translations compared on this page.
